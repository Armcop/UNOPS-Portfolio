About this portfolio

These case studies come from my work as Management Information Systems Officer at a credit reference bureau serving financial services clients. All client, staff and customer details have been removed and no real data appears here. Each case follows the same pattern: define the question, clean and reconcile the data, document the rules, and communicate the result plainly.

Case 1

Automated daily collections activity report

Managers needed a daily view of whether outcomes logged in the CRM were backed by real dialling activity, per officer and per hour, without an analyst building it by hand each evening.

What I did

  • Built a Python pipeline that loads PBX call records and CRM exports, filters to the report date, reconciles the two sources and writes a styled seven-tab Excel workbook: summary, officer activity (CRM vs PBX), hourly PBX activity, hourly CRM activity, accounts not called, and month-to-date payments.
  • Defined colour-coded coverage statuses so managers see problems at a glance.
  • Handled real-world data issues: phone numbers stored as imprecise floats (matched on the string field instead), files spanning several dates, trailing blank rows, comma-formatted numbers, and a morning-only window for Saturdays.
  • Used an authoritative officer-to-extension reference file instead of guessing from caller-ID text, and recalculated every workbook automatically to catch errors before sending.
PBX call records→CRM exports→Clean & normalise→Reconcile CRM vs PBX→7-tab workbook→Validate & email

Result

A fully automated daily report that replaced a manual process and gives leaders a consistent, comparable view, including repeat flags for officers whose activity swings sharply between days.

Case 2

Verifying a worksheet of accounts against call records

A worksheet of several hundred accounts needed checking against telephony records to see which had actually been called on the day, and what happened on the call.

  • Preserved the original population exactly (same number of rows in and out) and left the original eight columns untouched, adding nine verification columns beside them.
  • Matched primary and alternative phone numbers across several CDR files, accepting only qualifying outbound calls on the report date and keeping the longest match per row.
  • Classified each account by call duration into the four outcome categories above.
  • Stated the limits clearly in the cover note: CDR coverage ended mid-afternoon, recording flags are metadata rather than playback checks, and ownership was not inferred where the source had no owner field.

Result

A reproducible, auditable check where the method, exclusions and caveats are all written down, so reviewers know exactly what the numbers do and do not show.

Tools: Python, pandas, Excel

Case 3

Investigating a revenue discrepancy

A colleague's half-year figures did not agree with the master data. Where did the difference come from?

  • Traced one officer's records across the CRM and the telephony system to compare what each source showed.
  • Found that four officers in the colleague's sheets were missing from the current master roster, a clear data-integrity flag.
  • Reported the root cause as unconfirmed, setting out the evidence and open questions instead of asserting a theory the data could not support.

Result

A clear, honest written finding that let leadership decide what to verify next, with no unsupported claims.

Tools: Excel, Python, cross-source reconciliation

Case 4

Weekly executive intelligence brief

Senior leadership wanted one short weekly read on collections performance across several lenders.

  • Combined call volumes and connect rates by lender, a finance payment backlog, and a portfolio data anomaly flagged for verification.
  • Wrote findings in plain language with named action recommendations rather than raw tables.

Result

A repeatable weekly brief that gave the Group Managing Director a decision-ready summary.

Tools: Power BI, Excel, written briefing

Case 5

Multi-tab executive KPI dashboards

Leadership needed one consistent set of KPIs instead of separate, differently defined reports.

  • Designed a standardised KPI framework covering contact rates, conversion rates, collection rates and productivity.
  • Built multi-tab dashboards in Power BI and Excel with week-over-week trend comparisons and recommendations.

Result

Delivered every week for more than five consecutive months, giving leaders a stable baseline for comparison.

Tools: Power BI, Excel
